# 13 — MAXIS WHOLE-SYSTEM EXCELLENCE STANDARD

Status: LOCKED QUALITY DOCTRINE
Version: 1.0

## Cake → Icing → Ice Cream → Cherry → Star

### 1. Cake — foundation
Must fundamentally work: correct, useful, complete, reliable, logical, secure, well-architected.

### 2. Icing — refinement
Improve wording, UX, structure, visual hierarchy, accessibility, interaction quality, performance, clarity and consistency.

### 3. Ice Cream — delight
Add purposeful personality, micro-interactions, transitions, thoughtful surprises, emotional resonance and Naya humanity. Never add gimmicks for their own sake.

### 4. Cherry — memorable detail
Add the elegant touch or meaningful detail that creates “they thought of everything.” The cherry may be subtraction, a sentence, interaction, reveal, timing choice, or unseen intelligent logic.

### 5. Star — pinnacle
Ask what makes the whole creation feel complete, memorable and extraordinary. The Star is not necessarily another feature. It can be removal, simplicity, perfect wording, a reveal, an animation, Naya's voice, or effortless complexity.

## Star gate
Before declaring finished:
1. Does it work? Cake.
2. Is it exceptionally refined? Icing.
3. Is it delightful? Ice Cream.
4. Is there something memorable? Cherry.
5. Does it have the “holy shit, they thought of everything” quality? Star.

If Star is absent, continue evaluating. But do not add complexity merely to manufacture a Star.

## Guardrail
Never add complexity to create the appearance of excellence. A final layer must improve one or more of Beauty, Clarity, Usefulness, Trust, Accessibility, Delight, Effectiveness or Meaning. Otherwise cut it.

## Whole-system principle
Nothing is complete merely because individual parts are excellent. The integrated experience must work as a coherent whole.

Evaluate together:

- Purpose
- Human experience
- Psychology

## Nothing-left-unthought audit
Before production, deliberately ask:
- User: confusion, intimidation, abandonment, distrust, dishonesty, “not for me.”
- Psychology: judgment, defensiveness, overload, progress and motivation.
- Logic: ambiguous answer patterns, misleading scores, false diagnoses.
- Engineering: refresh, close/reopen, failed audio/AI/database, double submit, offline/interrupted state.
- Design: eye path, hierarchy, competing attention, repetition, obvious next action.
- Business: value before ask, offer relevance, honesty, manipulation risk, recommendation without purchase.
- Scale: larger question sets, other assessments, languages, industries, brands, models, Naya modes, organizations.

## Negative-space law
Master builders ask “What should we remove?”
Remove unnecessary buttons, paragraphs, steps, questions, animations and features.
Extra complexity must earn its place.

## Core equation
The parts should create synergy, not merely add up.
INTELLIGENCE + UTILITY + BEAUTY + SIMPLICITY + TRUST + EMOTION + PERFORMANCE + ACCESSIBILITY + EDUCATION + BUSINESS + SCALABILITY + DELIGHT → SYNERGY.

## Should-have-thought-of-that test
Before launch, anticipate reasonable “What about…?”, “Did you consider…?”, “What happens if…?” and “Why this way?” questions. Where feasible, the answer should be “already handled,” “tested,” or a clear rationale.

## Deepest rule
Leave the human better than you found them.
Success means the user understands more, can do more, feels more capable, knows what to do next, and experiences the product as beautiful and trustworthy.

## Experience over features
Stop asking only “What features should we add?” Ask “What experience should exist?”
Every section must earn its place by increasing understanding, trust, desire or action. Otherwise cut it.
